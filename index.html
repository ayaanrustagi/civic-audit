Leander & Cedar Park, Texas · Fall 2026 · An independent study

An AI news site said “dozens” of residents spoke. The minutes say three.

Citizen Portal publishes AI-written summaries and articles about local government meetings. I checked 1,999 individual facts from its coverage of four Leander-area public bodies against the official minutes, agendas and meeting video.

  • 1,999facts checked against the record
  • 55distinct errors, in 128 places
  • 15serious errors about votes, counts or official actions
  • 10 of 79articles with at least one serious error

By Ayaan Rustagi · Draft of 18 Nov 2026 · Explore every error · Read the paper

What I found

Most of what Citizen Portal wrote matched the record. In a random sample of claims both automatic checkers accepted, the reference review found 0 errors in 29. But the errors that exist are concentrated where they matter: names of officials and schools, how votes were taken, how many residents spoke, and the legal basis for closed sessions.

34 of the 55 distinct errors are misspelled names, many of them sound-alikes produced by speech recognition. Because Citizen Portal generates several articles from each meeting, one mistake is often copied into many places: the 55 distinct errors appear 128 times.

How readers are told. All 14 meeting pages carry the notice “The text on this page is AI generated… AI can make mistakes.” The two article pages checked for it on 17 Nov 2026 carry no such notice, and name the author only as the organization “Citizen Portal”.

Four cases

01

“Dozens”, twice in one month

Two Citizen Portal articles about Leander ISD board meetings opened with crowds that the record does not show. On 15 October, an article said “Dozens of community members” spoke; the approved minutes say: “Three citizens addressed the Board of Trustees.” (minutes). On 29 October, another said “Dozens of residents” urged the board to pause its school plan; the district’s captions show four people speaking (video, 16:16).

Why it matters: a count of public commenters is how residents and officials judge how much a decision is contested. Both are first-paragraph errors that a reader cannot catch without the record.

02

A roll call that wasn’t

For the Leander City Council special meeting of 22 September, Citizen Portal wrote “The motion carried on a roll call vote”. On the official video the presiding officer calls for a show of hands: “WE HAVE A MOTION AND A SECOND. ALL THOSE IN FAVOR? ALL RIGHT. THE MOTION CARRIES.” (video, 34:15). Only one of the three votes that night was a roll call.

Why it matters: how a vote is taken determines whether individual votes are on the record. Getting the procedure wrong misdescribes accountability.

03

One mishearing, 10 places

Leander ISD is repurposing Faubion Elementary. Citizen Portal’s meeting page for 1 October called it “Fabian”, and the misspelling then appeared in 3 articles generated from that meeting, 10 places in all, including a headline. Hisle Elementary became “Heisel” in 7 places the same way. The minutes spell both correctly (minutes).

Why it matters: 34 of the 55 distinct errors are names, mostly sound-alike spellings typical of speech recognition. Errors made once upstream are copied into every article downstream.

04

The wrong legal reason for a closed session

Texas law lets a council meet privately only for listed reasons. For Cedar Park’s 8 October meeting, Citizen Portal said the closed session concerned “personnel/confidential matters”. The minutes cite “Section § 551.071 (2) Consultation With Attorney” and real-property deliberation, not personnel (minutes).

Why it matters: the legal basis for a closed meeting is one of the few public checks on secrecy in local government.

How the audit works

  1. Collect, logged out

    14 Citizen Portal meeting pages (194 timeline segments) and the 79 articles linked to them, for four bodies, September to November 2026. For articles, only the headline, summary line and first paragraph were used. Pages were read at least 10 seconds apart, with no login and no Citizen Portal transcripts.

  2. Gather the record

    Official minutes and agendas (CivicClerk for Leander, BoardBook for Leander ISD, Cedar Park’s agenda portal) and the official video (Swagit, YouTube). Machine captions only locate moments; signed minutes and agendas decide names, votes, amounts and dates.

  3. Split into atomic claims

    Each summary, segment and article passage was split into single checkable facts, following the FActScore approach. Every claim must quote the exact words it came from; claims whose quote is not in the text were dropped.

  4. Two independent checkers

    GPT-6.1 Terra (Codex, high reasoning) judged all 1,999 claims against the matching record passages and had to quote the record. A second checker (1,401 claims by GPT-6 Astra, 435 by Claude Sonnet after a usage limit stopped the first model) re-checked every flagged item plus a fixed random 25% of the rest (seed 20261116), without seeing the first verdicts. Agreement: Cohen’s κ = 0.73 on 1,836 claims.

  5. Reference review

    Every claim either checker called contradicted, plus a random sample of claims both called supported, was read against the cited record and labelled with an error type and severity. A claim is an error only when the official document, or for spoken words the video, contradicts it. These labels were drafted by AI reviewers and are being confirmed by the author one by one.

  6. Honest statistics

    Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals that resample whole items (a segment or a summary), because claims from one item are not independent. Because flagged claims were heavily oversampled for review, no overall error rate is claimed.

Limits

  • Small sample: 14 meeting pages, 4 bodies, one season. Intervals are wide and results may not generalize to other places Citizen Portal covers.
  • The reference labels were drafted by AI reviewers. 263 of 294 target claims are labelled; the random supported sample has 29 of 60 done (0 errors, 95% upper bound 11.3% by item). Every label is pending the author’s confirmation.
  • No study-wide error rate is reported, because flagged claims were oversampled. Claims marked unclear or not found (391 and 221 by the first checker) are counted neither as errors nor as successes.
  • Minutes were not yet posted for 5 meetings; those were checked against agendas and video only. Some spoken-word claims rest on captions.
  • Only the first paragraph of each article was checked. Omissions (things the record shows that Citizen Portal left out) were not measured.
  • Citizen Portal pages can change after an error is reported. Snapshots are dated. Two candidate number errors were withdrawn after re-transcription showed Citizen Portal was right.

What Citizen Portal does well

  • Every meeting page links each summary to a time in the official video and says the text is AI generated. That transparency is what made this audit possible.
  • In the random sample of claims both checkers accepted, no errors were found (0 of 29).
  • Dates and characterizations were rarely flagged: the first checker flagged under 5% of claims of those types.
  • On 17 Nov 2026 Citizen Portal removed the sign-up prompt and began showing whole articles to logged-out readers.

About the author

Ayaan Rustagi is a high school junior in Leander, Texas. He designed and ran this audit to test whether AI-written coverage of his own city and school district matches the public record of the meetings it describes.

Ethics and AI use

  • Only public, logged-out pages and official public records were used.
  • Members of the public and students who spoke at meetings are never named. Officials and staff are named as they appear in the public record.
  • Citizen Portal was told about errors before publication and given a right of reply.
  • AI models were used to split text into claims, as checkers and to draft reference labels. They were measured against each other and against the record, and the author confirms every label by hand before the findings are final.
